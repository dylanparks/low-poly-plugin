# The head and face

## Proportions

| Check | Target | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Thirds: hairline-glabella, glabella-subnasale, subnasale-menton | about 1 : 1 : 1 | classical canon, clinical |
| The eyes | at half the head's height, crown to chin | classical canon |
| Rule of fifths: an eye's width, the gap between the eyes, the nose's width | about 1 : 1 : 1 | classical canon |
| Nasion to menton | 0.123 (ANSUR II menton-sellion, men) | survey |
| Head length / breadth | 0.200 / 0.154 | survey |
| Cheekbones across (bizygomatic) | 0.143 | survey |
| Nose wings across | about 0.035 (men's 3D-scan mean 34.9 mm); the bridge beside the eyes about 70% of it | scan studies |
| Mouth | about 0.054 across | measured on the build |

The first build's features sat 1.5 cm high, its hairline low and its chin short (the forehead third came to a third of
the others); lowering each face ring's front, not the skull's back, and raising the hairline fixed it.

## The profile

| Check | Target (men) |
|---|---|
| Lower lip to the E-line (nose tip to chin) | about 2-4 mm behind |
| Nasofacial angle | 30-40 degrees (about 30 rated ideal on men) |
| Nasomental angle | 120-132 degrees |
| Chin point (pogonion) against a vertical through the nasion | on the line or a little ahead |
| Dip under the lower lip | 4-6 mm deep |
| Nasolabial angle (the nose's underside to the upper lip) | 90-95 degrees |
| Brow-lips-chin convexity | 165-175 degrees |
| Nose tip to subnasale | about 21 mm |

The profile needs its own points: under the nose, the upper lip, the mouth's line, the lower lip, the dip, the chin's
point, under the chin. One point for both lips can't show two lips.

## The head's planes

Put vertices where a man's head turns from one plane to the next (Loomis, Reilly, Proko's planes of the head) and keep
them off the flats:
- the brow ridge 1.7 cm ahead of the socket; the temple's hollow behind the brow's outer end; the brow's outer end
  forward;
- the cheekbone where the face's front turns to the side, the widest point below the eyes;
- the cheek under it one plane falling back to the jaw;
- the jaw's angle a corner, the chin a box about 4 cm wide;
- the mouth's barrel standing off the cheek, meeting it at one vertex beside the nasolabial fold;
- the nose's bridge, sides, tip and base.

**The lower face's rows stay local** (midline to the barrel's side, three points each). Wrapped round the whole head they
crossed the cheek as five thin strips that the light drew as a band. Local rows cut the model from 711 to 670 vertices
with the profile unchanged.

**Folds that should be soft.** The mouth's corner into the cheek, the nose's wing into the cheek, the chin's side into
the cheek: under about 15 degrees. The nasolabial fold is painted, lightly; a dent for it in the mesh read as a gouge.

## The jaw and chin

- **The jaw's lower edge (the mandibular plane)**, menton to gonion, at about 25 ± 5 degrees to the Frankfort line (the eye
  socket's lower rim to the top of the ear canal; Tweed's FMA). At 14 degrees the first build's jaw ran nearly level back to
  a corner low on the neck: the lower face heavy, the line under the chin long. Below about 20 is the square, low-angle jaw.
- **The gonion** sits under the ear, about 1-2 cm below the lobe. The gonial angle (ramus to jaw body) is 120-125
  degrees in men.
- **Width.** Jaw to cheekbones about 0.82 on the first build. Perception studies rate 0.73-0.75 most attractive, but face
  height to jaw width pulls the other way, and a broad square jaw is the male head's mark in the drawing references. The
  jaw stays nearly as wide as its angle until it turns to the chin: points tapering early made a V from above.
- **The era's construction** (the reference game's hero, era context only): four midline points from the nose down (under
  the nose, the mouth, the chin's front, the menton); the chin a near-vertical front plane about 3 cm tall over a flat
  bottom; three points a side along the jaw; the shadow under it painted. The jaw read from the silhouette of a straight
  lower edge rising to a corner under the ear.

## The neck and throat

- **The cervicomental angle** (under the chin to the neck's front) 105-120 degrees, about 110: drop the neck ring's front
  below its sides, so the jaw's underside slopes down to the throat.
- **The strip from the jaw to the neck** mixes head and body vertices. It belongs wholly to one chart, or its triangles
  alternate between two paints (`texture.md`).

## Ears

| What | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 64.2 ± 4.5 mm (5th-95th 57-72) | ANSUR II |
| Breadth | 36.1 ± 3.0 mm, about 56% of the length | ANSUR II |
| Protrusion (mastoid to the most lateral point) | 23.2 ± 3.0 mm | ANSUR II |
| Helix to mastoid (the otoplasty norms) | 10-12 mm top, 16-18 middle, 20-22 lobe: measured to the mastoid, behind and below the ear where the head is narrower, so the lobe is not the part furthest from the midline | Nazarian 2011; Ali 2017 |
| Concha depth | about 15 mm | Ali 2017 |
| Height | the top at the brow's outer end, the lobe at the nose's tip | Proko; Nazarian; Ali |
| Front to back | the tragus about one ear's length (55-70 mm) behind the eye socket's outer rim, at the back of the jaw | Naumann 2007; Proko |
| Tilt | about 20 degrees back from vertical (15-30) | Nazarian; Ali |
| Flare from the head | 20-30 degrees | Nazarian; Naumann |
| Across the ears | about 18.5-19.5 cm on a 15.4 cm head (head breadth is taken above the ears; they stand 1.5-2 cm past it a side; the first build 18.8) | estimate from the survey; the build |

- **Build it as a cupped shell, its own piece.** A seven-point helix outline, a point on the concha's floor (4 mm off the
  skin against a rim 12-23 mm off: a bowl about 15 mm deep), and the back running to two root points just under the skin,
  high and low behind the concha. The shell meets the head along a base about 4 cm long. One sunk point made a stalk.
- **Seen from the front, a C widest in its upper half**, the lobe tucked toward the jaw. Square off the skin under each
  rim point, the first build stands 13 mm at the top, 20-22 at the back's upper half and middle, 20 at the lower back and
  12 at the lobe: 8.9, 9.4, 9.2, 8.6 and 8.3 cm from the midline. With the most standoff low, the ears hung like fangs;
  too little, and they hid behind the cheeks.
- **Measure the skin under it on the built mesh.** A guessed skin width under the lobe (7.2 cm where the skin was 6.6)
  stood the lower ear 29 mm off the head.
- **Lit as part of the head.** Blend the ear's normals with the head's nearest skin (fully at the root, about half over
  the shell), and paint its back and the fold behind it in the head's shaded skin (`texture.md`).
- **The antihelix's Y, the scapha, the canal and the tragus are painted.** A rim's real thickness (5-8 mm) would cost
  about 7 vertices and 14 triangles a side.
- **One parameter set** (the tragion, length, breadth, protrusion, tilt and a tip offset) makes a race's ear
  (`races.md`).

## Eyes, brows and mouth: paint

**Brows (masculine).**
- **Placement.** On the bony rim over the eye socket, not above the rim (a woman's sits a few millimetres above it). Heavy, nearly straight, peaking two-thirds out over the
  iris's outer edge (a peak toward the nose reads as surprise).
- **Ends.** The head starts over the nose's wing and the inner eye corner; the tail ends on the line from the wing through
  the outer eye corner.
- **Shape.** About 8 mm thick at the head, tapering to 4 mm. The lower edge firm, the upper feathered, the hairs rising at
  the head and lying outward along the body.
- **Mask.** The brows are in the hair colour's mask, exactly as painted. A mask wider than the paint left a pale halo on
  dark skin.

**Eyes.**
- **Life size.** A person's eye is painted at its real size. The animal rule of painting an eye 1.5-2 times its size
  (`lowpoly-animals/references/eyes.md`) is for a side-facing eye read from far off, and doesn't apply to a face.
- **The opening.** About 10 mm tall and 31 long. The iris (11.6 mm) sits a little high, so the upper lid covers its top:
  relaxed and slightly hooded. A 14 mm opening showed the whole iris, a stare.
- **The white.** Never pure white: greyer at the corners, in the lid's shadow.
- **Iris and catchlight.** A dark limbal ring round the iris. One sharp catchlight at the iris's top centre; on a mirrored
  face chart it must sit on the centre line, or the two eyes' highlights point apart.
- **Wet highlights.** A pink inner corner and a light line along the lower lid.

**Mouth.**
- **The line.** Nearly level on the mesh's mouth row (a line set below the row drew a frown), with a small shade at each
  corner.
- **The lips.** The upper lip, facing down, a shade darker; the lower lip lit.
- **Front faces only.** Paint the mouth's lines and shadows only where the skin faces forward. Placed by height alone,
  they landed on the jaw's underside as lines across the throat.

## The era's level of painted detail

Measured on the reference game's young male faces (twelve skins on one head layout), as brightness against the cheek. Era
context: our renderer also lights the mesh, so aim for the lighter end of each range.

| Feature | Painted | Extent |
|---|---|---|
| Lid line, lashes, pupil | 0.05-0.30 | about a texel |
| Eye socket | 0.55-0.80 | the brow down to below the lower lid, about 1.7 eye-widths across, deepest toward the bridge |
| The white | 0.65-0.87 | never brighter than the cheek |
| Under the brow; the cheek below the eye | 1.00-1.05; 1.03-1.07 | lit |
| Under the nose | 0.45-0.50 | |
| Upper lip / lower lip | 0.75-0.90 / 0.85-1.00 | |
| Mouth line | 0.35-0.55, red 1.25-1.65 times the cheek's | 2-3 texels: the colour sits in the line, not the lips |
| Under the lower lip | 0.50-0.65 | |

The style is small sharp lines inside broad soft shadows. A face with the lines but none of the soft shadows reads flat
round the eyes, and its whites glare.
